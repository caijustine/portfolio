The elephant in the room

What about AI?

easy:

Everybody either hates AI or loves it. There is no in-between, and Thanksgiving dinner has proven that. So I’ll just say it: I love it. I walked into this field right as AI did, so no, I don’t have ten years of hard coding under my belt (shocking, I know). I love hard coding, I’m still learning it, and part of me wishes I’d shown up back when that was the only option. But the industry has changed, and AI now writes about 42% of all committed code. Time is everything… but so is quality.

So the real challenge is building the best, cleanest version of something in the smartest way possible, without spending years hard coding it first. Fast is great. Fast and broken is just a faster way to cry. Getting it right is where I take pride in my work, and it’s what I’m always working to get better at.

And let me just say, AI is straight-up magic. Whether that’s good magic or dark magic, lol, that’s your call. You tell it what you want and bam, you’ve created another dimension. But AI is only as good as the person using it… and some days I worry for mine. So I put the work into knowing exactly what to ask for and double-checking everything it hands back, because it will happily build the wrong thing beautifully.

I could talk about AI forever, but considering I’ve already crammed what should’ve been about five pages of text onto one, I think I’ve made my point. You’re welcome (I’m sorry lol).

How it helps me learn

When I don’t understand something, I ask AI to explain it. Then I ask why. Then I ask again, like a toddler, until it finally clicks. It’s the most patient tutor I’ve ever had, it has never once sighed at me, and it’s a big reason I went from knowing absolutely nothing to shipping real projects in a year. (And to any of my teachers reading this: you helped a lot too. You just needed more sleep than AI does.)

How I check its work

AI is fast. It’s also confidently wrong sometimes (which, yea okay same… and I wouldn’t believe you if you said you never have… I can feel it through the screen). So I read every change before I keep it, run it to make sure it actually works, and make it walk me through anything I couldn’t have written myself. I’m also learning more ways to double-check it, like writing unit tests and setting up AI skills that review my work before anything gets the go-ahead. If I can’t explain the code, it doesn’t ship. No vibes-only commits.

AI I’ve built into my projects

I don’t just use AI… I put it to work. My Daily Work Ledger uses Claude to write daily and weekly reports from only what I logged, and it’s been told very firmly not to make anything up. Nebula has a chat assistant that answers questions about a class’s shared resources and streams its answers back live, so nobody has to dig through a pile of links to find one thing.
